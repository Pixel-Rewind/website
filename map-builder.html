Map builder

Community tool · online matches

Every online match is fought on a board. Draw your own here: who starts where, which ground each player owns, where the holes and the monsters are. Save it as a file and send it to us.

  1. Start from a blank board, one of the examples below, or a map file you saved earlier.
  2. Draw. Pick a tool, then click or drag on the board. The list under the board tells you what still needs fixing.
  3. Send. Download the file and e-mail it to us. We try every map out before it is used in a real match.

Nothing leaves your browser until you send it. This page uploads nothing. Your drawing is kept on this device, so you can close the page and come back to it.

How a map plays
What the pieces are
  • Territory is the ground a player owns when the match starts. Walking over your own ground is faster than crossing anyone else's. Give everyone the same amount unless you mean it otherwise.
  • Castle. Every player has one castle, 2×2 cells. A hero that dies comes back there. Win by bringing another player's princess home to your castle.
  • Hero. Every player has exactly one start cell for their hero. It can be on their own castle or next to it, never on someone else's.
  • Princess. She starts inside her castle. You can move her onto the board, or leave a castle empty.
  • Monsters start next to the player you give them to and fight for them.
  • Holes are cells nothing can stand on, be built on or be dropped on. Heroes walk around them.
Deathmatch and Princess Rush
Deathmatch: a board with no castles. Nobody respawns, a hero that dies is out, and the last one standing wins. There are no princesses on such a board. Switch to it under “Board settings → Mode”.
Princess Rush: a room mode where every castle starts empty and one neutral princess waits somewhere on the board. Whoever brings her home first wins. Place her with the “Neutral princess” tool; without one she starts on the free cell nearest the middle. The other modes ignore her.
Limits, and what cannot be drawn
Each side is 6 to 32 cells (the game's own boards are 10 to 12; much bigger ones have not been tried in a real match). At most 32 monsters per board, one castle, hero and princess per player. Every piece needs a cell of its own and stays off holes; princesses and monsters stay off castles.
Items are not part of a map: the game drops them during the match, and your item rules decide where.
Sprites on the board: Super Retro World by Gif (@gif_not_jif), Noiracide (@Noiracide) and Romi (@DessRomaric). They are stand-ins: the game draws its own. The four players' colours here only tell them apart; in a match every player keeps their own.
